Where you work

Your interface is the Claude desktop app on your Windows laptop. It has two modes, and you will use both.

Cowork

Everyday knowledge work: reading and drafting email, research, documents, spreadsheets. It works across your apps and files. Think of it as the front office.

Claude Code

Opens inside your hub folder. It builds tools, runs agents and keeps everything filed in GitHub. This is the workshop floor.

Phone and cloud

The phone app captures ideas and checks results. Scheduled agents run in the cloud, so a Monday report arrives even when your laptop is closed.

The one rule that makes it all work: if it matters, it lives in a file in your hub. Chats are scratch paper. Files are the drawings everyone builds from, including AIs from other companies.

GitHub, in plain terms

GitHub is a plan room for your work. Your laptop holds a working copy. GitHub holds the master set: every revision ever made, who made it, and a note saying why.

Why bother

You never lose work. You can undo anything. You get the same setup on any computer. You can share a tool with a link, and the backlog sits right next to the thing it is about.

What never goes in

Client data, passwords and employer files. Those stay in your work systems, and agents reach them through connectors. GitHub holds the tools, not the cargo.

The process engine

Every workshop needs a way to turn "that should be better" into "it is better." Software people call this a workflow. Business people call it continuous improvement. Either way, it is what makes the setup improve each week instead of slowly going stale.

Keep two lists apart. Work to-dos, like "call Mrs. Chan back," live in Outlook or To Do. Backlog items, like "make the call brief better," live in GitHub. Mix them and you bury both.

Agents

An agent is a job description in a text file. It names the job, the tools it may use, what it must never do, and what good output looks like. Write one and you have hired someone. Edit the file and you have retrained them.

---
name: follow-up-finder
description: Finds promises I made in email and call notes that I haven't kept.
tools: Outlook (read), Calendar (read)
---
Every weekday at 7:30:
1. Read my sent email and call notes from the last 10 days.
2. Find promises: "I'll send", "let me check", "by Friday".
3. Check whether I followed through.
4. Save a short list to outputs/follow-ups/ and draft the replies.
Never send email. Never change the CRM.

To add one, ask Claude Code: "Create an agent that…" It writes the file. You read it, adjust it and commit it. You can also install ready-made agents from the shared toolkit.

Three ways an agent starts

You ask

"Find my overdue follow-ups." It runs now, while you watch or get on with something else.

On a schedule

Routines run in Anthropic's cloud with your laptop closed. Scheduled tasks run on your laptop when they need your local files.

When something happens

A new issue, a finished call or a message from another app sets it off. This is the most advanced of the three, so it comes later.

Connectors and plugins

Anyone who has built cabinets knows the 32 mm system: standard hole spacing means any maker's hinge fits any maker's cabinet. MCP, the Model Context Protocol, is that standard for AI. Any app that offers an MCP server can plug into any AI tool that speaks MCP, and Claude, Gemini, ChatGPT and most others now do.

PartWhat it isExample
MCPThe standard plug shape itself.The 32 mm hole pattern
ConnectorOne app plugged in through MCP, usually with a single sign-in.Outlook and calendar, SharePoint, Google Drive, Salesforce, GitHub
MCP serverThe app's side of the plug. You can build your own when no connector exists.A small server over a public open-data source, or over a firm's internal client list
SkillA recipe card: how to do one task, sometimes with a script attached."Write a client letter in our house style"
PluginA boxed kit of skills, agents and connectors that installs together.The shared toolkit
ExtensionPuts Claude inside another program.Claude in Chrome, Excel, PowerPoint

Is MCP being replaced? No. It has become the common standard across vendors. The newer piece is skills, which are often the lighter choice when you only need instructions and a script rather than a live connection to an app.

Some call-recording tools can only be reached through third-party MCP bridges. Connecting those, or a CRM, means company data, so get approval first.

Other AIs

Your hub is plain text files, which is what makes it portable. Claude reads CLAUDE.md, Google's agent reads GEMINI.md, and OpenAI's Codex reads AGENTS.md. Make AGENTS.md the real file and the others one-line pointers to it, and all three follow the same instructions, skills and connectors.

Why let them coexist

A second opinion. Ask a different model to check a client letter or the pricing math. Different models have different blind spots.

Budget. If you already pay for Google AI Pro, its allowance is extra capacity for big jobs.

Resilience. Vendors change their products. In June 2026 Google retired Gemini CLI for personal accounts and moved people to Antigravity CLI. Setups built on plain files moved over easily.

Your employer's choice. Your company may standardize on one vendor. Your files and habits come with you.

Advanced: foreman and subcontractor

  1. Claude plans the job and writes a handoff file: the exact task, the files involved and how to check the result.
  2. Claude runs Google's agent in the background on that task, using your Google allowance.
  3. Claude reviews the result and runs the checks.
  4. You approve, and the change is committed.

This is worth it for big, well-defined jobs. It adds moving parts, so leave it until the basics feel easy.

House rules and first steps

House rules

  • Agents read and draft freely. Anything that leaves the building, like an email, a CRM change or a published page, waits for your click.
  • Client and employer data stays in your work systems and never goes in GitHub.
  • Every automated run leaves a note in the logs folder.
  • Tax and legal agents analyze and draft. The professional signs.
  • Use company-approved accounts for company data. Until you have one, start with work that uses only public information.

First steps

  1. Install: one script sets up Git, GitHub and Claude Code. Allow about 20 minutes, ideally on a call with whoever is setting you up.
  2. Make your hub from the template. Claude interviews you to write your AGENTS.md.
  3. Build a first agent that uses public data only, such as the industry watch or the rules and rates report.
  4. Put it on a schedule.
  5. Start the loop: log every annoyance for two weeks.
  6. Add connectors once your employer approves.
Prepared October 2026. Product names and features change quickly. The files-first approach is the part that lasts.
